The whole game

Hold to pull in. Release to push out.

Both orbs are drawn toward the beam that kills them. Let go and they drift back to the walls. Every gate is a decision about how close you are willing to get — and a run lasts seconds, so the next attempt starts before you have finished being annoyed about the last one.

  • One thumb. No buttons, no menus, nothing to read mid-run.
  • Gates arrive locked to the sixteenth-note grid, not to a timer.
  • The arena scales, so a phone and a 13-inch iPad play at identical difficulty.
  • Landscape, offline, no account, nothing to sign up for.

The soundtrack

There is no music file. There is a synthesiser.

Every sample you hear is computed while you play — six voices rendered one frame at a time, with no allocation and no audio assets in the bundle at all. A 28-bar score in E minor, 448 sixteenth notes, not a single rest among them. The tempo climbs from 150 to 178 BPM as the field speeds up, and it never falls back.

Three ways to lose

Pick your patience.

NORMAL
Slow start · builds

Opens gently and accelerates for nearly two minutes. The mode where a run can actually go somewhere.

EXTREME
No warm-up · full speed

Starts at a pace Normal takes a minute to reach. Most runs end in seconds.

IMPOSSIBLE
Split control

The screen halves. Top drives the top orb, bottom drives the other. Two thumbs, one beam.

Earn the palette

Every gate counts, even on a run that scores zero.

Skins unlock from lifetime gates cleared, and each one retints the whole game — orbs, lanes, obstacles, and the beam itself.

One more go

A continue you can earn. Once.

Die and you can watch an ad to resume with the score intact. Once per run, and no more — this is a score-attack game, and a continue that can be bought repeatedly turns the leaderboard into a measure of patience rather than skill.

A one-time purchase removes the ads entirely.

How it was built

Measured, not guessed.

Most of the decisions in this game came from checking something rather than trusting an instinct. Several of them overturned the instinct.

The soundtrack was tested against research

The score was written to sit on the memorable side of an earworm study. The first attempt was measured against the paper, landed on the wrong side of it, and was thrown away rather than shipped.

A simulation got the game wrong

A bot played thousands of runs and never once died on the centre line, which suggested the mechanic was decorative. Real players died to it constantly. The bot had been written not to — the prediction was an assumption wearing a result's clothes.

One line was ending runs early

Three quarters of centre-line deaths scored exactly zero. The tap that began a run was also counting as a pull, so players died a third of a second in, before a single gate had appeared.

The colours passed a test and still failed

Two difficulty accents cleared a colour-distance check and looked identical anyway. The metric weighted brightness over hue, and hue is what an eye reads at speed. The metric was replaced.
